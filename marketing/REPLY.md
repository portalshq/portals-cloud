# REPLY PLAYBOOK

First Reply
Rule
Don't pitch. Mirror their pain. Ask one question. Reply within 30 minutes.
If they name a pain
They say: reproducing work / finding the original
reproducing shipped work — is that mostly on client extension requests,
or when you're iterating internally on a campaign?
They say: managing volume / keeping up with demand
volume — is the bottleneck on the generation side,
or keeping track of what's been approved and what hasn't?
They say: knowledge loss / someone leaving
that's the one that costs the most quietly.
how many people carry the context for an average production — is it one, or spread across a few?
If they ask what this is
They say: what do you do exactly?
portals is a production repository for AI-native creative teams.
every asset your team generates gets a permanent record — what it is,
how it was made, and every version it passed through.
when a project ships, that knowledge stays.

where does your current workflow break most often?
If they say not relevant
They say: not for us / not right now
appreciated — when would be the right time to check back, if at all?

Qualify
Collect these 5 signals across 2–3 exchanges — not as a questionnaire
1. Specific incident
Don't accept category. "We sometimes struggle with versioning" ≠ qualified.
"We re-did six assets for a campaign because the prompts were in someone's personal Midjourney account" = qualified.
Ask: "Can you walk me through the last time this actually hurt?"
2. Dollar cost
Already seeded in E1. If they gave a number, they're qualified.
If they deflected: "Ballpark — is this a thousands-per-quarter problem or a tens-of-thousands problem?"
If they deflect again: pain isn't at budget level yet.
3. Who else is involved
"Is this your call, or would someone else need to be part of the conversation?"
One person = fast path. Multiple stakeholders = map the org before the call.
4. What they've tried
"What have you done to solve this so far?"
Tried nothing = pain isn't sharp enough.
Tried Notion / Drive / Frame.io and it failed = actively shopping, timing is perfect.
5. What a yes looks like
Ask on the call: "If this solves exactly what you described, what would need to be true for you to move forward?"
Their answer surfaces the criteria and the objections before they become blockers.

Disqualify
Stop early if you see these. Don't persuade past them.
"We're still figuring out our AI workflow"
Too early. No pain yet.
Park in 90-day nurture. Send Production Memory Guide.
"We use [one tool] for everything"
No complexity, no repository problem.
Wrong ICP. Remove from sequence.
"I'd need to get five people aligned"
Enterprise motion. Wrong channel.
Ask who the internal champion is. Reframe as enterprise pilot.
No dollar figure after two asks
Pain is felt operationally, not at budget level.
Not a near-term deal. Nurture only.
"We don't really use AI generation tools yet"
Wrong ICP entirely.
Remove. Don't educate the market at this stage.

The Call
How to offer it
After 2–3 engaged email exchanges:
"Would it make sense to spend 15 minutes on this?"

Not: "Can I schedule a demo." Not: "Would you like to see the product."
15 minutes. Not a demo. Audio first, not video.
Call structure — 15 minutes
0–2 min
Confirm their pain in their words
"Before I say anything about the product — you mentioned [their pain]. Is that still the right thing to be talking about?"
Let them restate it. You're calibrating, not presenting.
2–8 min
Qualification questions
Walk the five signals. Conversational, not interrogation.
Listen for the specific incident. Get the dollar number if you haven't.
Ask who else is involved. Ask what they've tried.
8–12 min
Map their pain to the product briefly
"Here's what I'm hearing — [restate pain in their words]. What portals does is give you a permanent record of every production decision, so when the client asks for more, you're extending from the original, not rebuilding."
One statement. Then stop. Let them react.
12–15 min
Pilot proposal
"Would it make sense to scope a pilot around [their specific workflow they described]?"
If yes: move to close. If uncertain: ask what would need to be true.

Close
The pilot proposal
Use this language after a qualified call. Verbatim or close to it.
"Here's what I'd suggest: a 4-week pilot scoped around [their workflow they described]. We'd get the first production record established within 48 hours, and we'd agree on what success looks like before we start — not after. It's $5,000, and that credits toward the annual agreement if you sign within 14 days of the final review. Worst case you get a clear deployment recommendation. Want me to put together a scope?"
If they're shopping other solutions
"What else are you looking at?"
Not defensive — you want their frame.

"What would make one option the obvious choice over the others?"
Let them set the criteria. Show portals meets the criteria they named in their own words.
If they need internal approval
"Who else needs to be part of this conversation before it moves forward?"
Get them to introduce you, or offer to send a one-pager they can forward.
Do not let the deal go dark waiting for an internal update. Set a specific date: "When should I check back with you?"
If they say 'send me more info'
This is a soft no. Respond: "Happy to — what specific question should it answer?"
If they can't answer that, the interest isn't real. Park in nurture.
If they answer specifically, send that and only that.
